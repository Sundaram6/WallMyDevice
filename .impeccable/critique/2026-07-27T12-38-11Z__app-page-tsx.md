### Overall Impression
The application excels at providing a tactile, generative wallpaper studio with excellent state persistence. However, it risks overwhelming casual users with its dense mobile layout and complex initial onboarding, and has potential contrast issues with its hardcoded color scheme. The single biggest opportunity is refining the onboarding flow to ease users into the complex studio tools.

### What's Working
- **Context-Aware Defaults:** Smart viewport detection perfectly defaults to iPhone 16 Pro or iPad Air 11 frames.
- **Robust Persistence:** Excellent use of localStorage and hash routing to seamlessly save and share states (recipes).
- **Accessibility Integration:** The `AccessibilityPreviewBar` directly in the editor flow shows a standout commitment to inclusive design.

### Priority Issues
- **[P1] Mobile Density**: The bottom sheet for mobile might become overly dense and difficult to navigate if the `ControlPanel` has many parameters. This hurts touch target usability on small screens. Fix: Refine the mobile layout spacing and grouping for smaller devices. (Suggested command: `/impeccable adapt`)
- **[P1] Onboarding Mental Model**: New users might be overwhelmed by the "Studio" vs "Archive" mental model upon first drop-in. This increases cognitive load and abandonment risk. Fix: Introduce a guided first-run flow or use the Archive view as a visual template picker before entering the Studio. (Suggested command: `/impeccable onboard`)
- **[P1] Hardcoded Color Scheme Contrast**: The highly nuanced beige/warm grey palette (`#F3EFE6`, `#8A8579`) uses hardcoded hexes that might conflict with system dark modes or have low contrast in bright environments. Fix: Migrate hardcoded hexes to dynamic theme tokens to fully support dark mode. (Suggested command: `/impeccable colorize`)

### Persona Red Flags
**The Casual Browser**: Might find the sheer number of dials (grain, blur, overlays, palettes) intimidating if they just want a quick wallpaper. May abandon if they can't instantly generate a pleasing result without manual tweaking.
**The Power User**: Might find the color palette too "soft" or low-contrast compared to standard dark mode developer tools, and will notice if hardcoded hexes break their system dark mode preference.

### Minor Observations
- The mix of Tailwind utility classes and inline styles (for accessibility filters) is pragmatic.
- A debounced `setTimeout` (400ms) for saving state is a nice performance touch.

### Questions to Consider
- Does hardcoding hex colors like `#F3EFE6` in `className` conflict with the `(prefers-color-scheme: dark)` observer you're running? How does the "dark" mode state in `useEditorStore` actually propagate?
- Could the "archive" view serve as a more visual onboarding flow (e.g., picking a template) before dropping users into the complex "studio"?
